Old vs New Tax Regime FY 2025-26: Which Saves You More?

By Calqin Team • Published: 20 September 2026 • 7 min read

The Choice That Costs ₹30,000 If You Get It Wrong

Every January, HR sends an email asking you to declare tax regime for the next financial year. Most employees glance at it, pick one, and move on. That five-second decision can cost ₹20,000-40,000 in tax.

Consider two colleagues at the same company, both earning ₹12 lakh. Anjali picks the new regime. Her colleague Rohit picks the old regime and claims ₹3.5 lakh in deductions (80C + 80D + HRA + home loan interest). At year-end, Rohit's tax is ₹35,000 lower than Anjali's — same salary, same company, different decision. The difference wasn't in their income. It was in the regime they picked.

This calculator answers one question for your specific numbers: which regime pays less tax? Enter your income, deductions, and it compares both side by side.

How the Two Regimes Actually Differ

The new regime offers lower tax rates but almost no deductions. The old regime has higher rates but allows you to subtract investments, insurance premiums, HRA, and home loan interest from your taxable income.

Think of it as two separate deals the government offers:

  • New regime: "Pay lower rates, but we won't count your investments."
  • Old regime: "Pay higher rates, but we'll subtract your 80C, 80D, HRA, and home loan interest first."

The math works in the new regime's favour if your deductions are small (under ₹1.5 lakh). The old regime wins if your deductions cross ₹3-4 lakh — especially for home loan borrowers and high HRA claimants.

FY 2025-26 Tax Slabs

New Regime:

  • ₹0 – ₹3,00,000 — Nil
  • ₹3,00,001 – ₹6,00,000 — 5%
  • ₹6,00,001 – ₹9,00,000 — 10%
  • ₹9,00,001 – ₹12,00,000 — 15%
  • ₹12,00,001 – ₹15,00,000 — 20%
  • Above ₹15,00,000 — 30%

Old Regime:

  • ₹0 – ₹2,50,000 — Nil (₹3,00,000 for senior citizens)
  • ₹2,50,001 – ₹5,00,000 — 5%
  • ₹5,00,001 – ₹10,00,000 — 20%
  • Above ₹10,00,000 — 30%

Both regimes get the ₹50,000 standard deduction for salaried employees. Plus 4% health and education cess on the final tax.

Section 87A rebate: In the new regime, income up to ₹7 lakh is effectively tax-free (rebate of ₹25,000 applies). In the old regime, the rebate only covers income up to ₹5 lakh. So if you earn ₹7 lakh and have no deductions, the new regime gives you zero tax.

Notice how sensitive the choice is to deductions. A ₹50,000 swing in the deduction column can flip the answer. This is why "which regime is better" has no universal answer — only your numbers can decide.

Real Examples at Different Income Levels

Example 1 — ₹6 Lakh income (entry level)
With standard deduction, taxable income is ₹5.5 lakh in both regimes. Under the new regime, tax is only ₹6,500 (5% of ₹1.5 lakh above ₹3L + cess). Under old regime, tax is ₹15,600 (5% of ₹2.5L above ₹2.5L + cess). New regime saves ~₹9,000.
But if you claim ₹1.5 lakh 80C, old regime taxable income drops to ₹4 lakh, and tax becomes zero (rebate under 87A). Old regime wins with ₹6,500 savings.

Example 2 — ₹20 Lakh income (senior professional)
New regime tax: ₹3,05,400 (30% slab hits above ₹15L). Old regime tax: ₹3,62,400 with no deductions. New regime saves ₹57,000.
But with ₹4 lakh in deductions (80C + 80D + HRA + home loan), old regime taxable drops to ₹15.5L. Tax becomes ₹2,92,400 — ₹13,000 lower than the new regime. Old regime wins — but barely.

Example 3 — ₹50 Lakh income (business owner, salaried CEO)
New regime tax: ₹11,82,900. Old regime tax with ₹5 lakh deductions: ₹11,22,900. With deductions, old regime saves ₹60,000.
At this income, the difference is smaller as a percentage of income. Both regimes are within 5% of each other. Deductions matter less, rates matter more.

Example 4 — Freelancer with business income
Freelancers have a one-time switch option. If they pick old regime in FY 2025-26, they can't switch back. This is why most freelancers pick new regime by default — the flexibility cost of opting old is high. Unless deductions are substantial and consistent, new regime is safer for freelancers.

Common Mistakes While Choosing

  • Picking new regime by default. Since the new regime is the default, many employees don't actively choose. HR sends the form, they don't fill it, and end up in the new regime even if old was better. If you have ₹3+ lakh deductions, this silent default costs you ₹20,000-40,000.
  • Assuming old regime is always better if you invest. Investing in 80C doesn't automatically make old regime better. If your total deductions are only ₹1.5 lakh (just the 80C limit), the new regime's lower rates still win at most income levels. You need ₹3-4 lakh in deductions to make old regime worthwhile.
  • Forgetting that HRA + 80C + 80D stack. Many users calculate only one deduction and conclude. The real calculation adds 80C, 80D, HRA, home loan interest, and NPS 80CCD(1B) together. A ₹12 lakh earner in a rented house with home loan can easily cross ₹4 lakh in deductions.
  • Not factoring employer NPS contribution. In the new regime, employer NPS contribution (up to 14% of basic) is still deductible under 80CCD(2). Users often miss this — it's the one big deduction that works in the new regime.

When This Calculator Doesn't Apply

The comparison works for most salaried taxpayers. But some cases need more nuance:

  • Business income with presumptive taxation. If you're a freelancer under 44ADA or business owner under 44AD, the regime choice interacts with presumptive income rules. Talk to a CA.
  • Capital gains income. LTCG on equity is taxed at 12.5% regardless of regime. The regime comparison applies only to salary and other regular income.
  • Foreign income with DTAA. Double taxation avoidance agreements add complexity the calculator doesn't cover.
  • Multiple house properties. If you own more than two properties, the deemed rental income rules change, which affects home loan interest deduction.

For these cases, consult a CA. The calculator handles standard salaried scenarios.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which tax regime is better for ₹12 lakh income?
It depends on your deductions. At ₹12 lakh income with no deductions, the new regime saves roughly ₹78,000. But with ₹3-4 lakh in deductions (80C + 80D + HRA + home loan), the old regime saves ₹20,000-30,000. Run both scenarios with your actual numbers.

Can I switch between old and new regimes every year?
Salaried employees can switch every year at the time of filing ITR. Business owners with business income can switch only once in their lifetime. This means a salaried person can choose old regime one year and new regime next year, whichever saves more.

Is the new regime now the default?
Yes. From FY 2023-24 onward, the new regime is the default for all taxpayers. If you want the old regime, you must explicitly opt for it at the time of filing ITR. Many employees forget this and end up in the new regime even when old was better for them.

What deductions are allowed in the new regime?
Very few. Standard deduction of ₹50,000 for salaried, employer NPS contribution under 80CCD(2), and a few others. The major ones — 80C (₹1.5L), 80D (health insurance), HRA, home loan interest — are NOT allowed in the new regime. This is why people with big deductions prefer the old regime.

Does the new regime have any rebate benefit?
Yes. Under Section 87A, income up to ₹7 lakh is effectively tax-free in the new regime (rebate of ₹25,000 applies). In the old regime, the rebate only works up to ₹5 lakh. So if your income is below ₹7 lakh, the new regime gives zero tax — even without any deductions.

Which regime is better for senior citizens?
For senior citizens (60+), the old regime has an extra benefit — higher basic exemption limit (₹3 lakh vs ₹2.5 lakh). Plus 80D deduction for health insurance and 80TTB for interest income up to ₹50,000. Unless total deductions are under ₹1.5 lakh, the old regime usually wins for seniors.
