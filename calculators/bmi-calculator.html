What is BMI?

BMI (Body Mass Index) is a measure of body fat based on your weight and height. It's used worldwide as a quick screening tool to categorize adults into underweight, normal weight, overweight, or obese. BMI doesn't measure body fat directly, but it correlates well with more direct measures.

BMI Formula

BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ Height (m)²

Example: Weight 70 kg, Height 1.70 m → BMI = 70 ÷ (1.70 × 1.70) = 24.2

BMI Categories (WHO)

  • Below 18.5 — Underweight
  • 18.5 – 24.9 — Normal weight
  • 25.0 – 29.9 — Overweight
  • 30.0 and above — Obese

Limitations of BMI

BMI has some limitations you should know:

  • Doesn't distinguish between muscle and fat — athletes may show "overweight"
  • Doesn't account for age, gender, or body composition
  • Not accurate for pregnant women
  • Not suitable for children (use BMI-for-age percentile)

What to Do With Your BMI

If your BMI is outside the normal range, don't panic. BMI is just a screening tool. Consult a doctor or nutritionist for a full assessment. Lifestyle factors like diet, exercise, sleep, and stress matter just as much.
