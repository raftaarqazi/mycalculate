BMI for Children — Important Note

BMI for children is calculated the same way as adults, but interpretation is completely different. Children's body composition changes rapidly with age and gender. A pediatrician must interpret BMI using age-and-gender-specific growth charts (percentiles).

Why Adult BMI Categories Don't Apply to Kids

An adult BMI of 18 is "normal" but for a 5-year-old, it may be "underweight". Children's BMI is compared against other children of the same age and gender using CDC or WHO growth charts.

BMI Categories for Children (Percentile-Based)

  • Below 5th percentile: Underweight
  • 5th to 85th percentile: Healthy weight
  • 85th to 95th percentile: Overweight
  • Above 95th percentile: Obese

BMI Formula (Same as Adults)

BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ Height (m)²

What Affects a Child's BMI?

  • Age and gender
  • Growth spurts
  • Muscle mass (athletic kids may show higher BMI)
  • Genetic factors
  • Diet and physical activity

When to Consult a Pediatrician

  • BMI is below 5th percentile (underweight)
  • BMI is above 85th percentile (overweight)
  • Sudden weight gain or loss
  • Eating or activity concerns
  • Any persistent health issue

Healthy Habits for Kids

  • Balanced diet with fruits and vegetables
  • At least 60 minutes of physical activity daily
  • Limited screen time (max 2 hours/day)
  • 8-11 hours of sleep depending on age
  • Regular health checkups
